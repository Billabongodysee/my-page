From Log to Slab: Where Our Wood Comes From

Every cutting board starts long before it ever touches the shop. Most of our hardwood comes from local sawmills and small-batch suppliers who kiln-dry their lumber properly — that drying step matters more than people think. Wood that isn't dried evenly will warp, crack, or cup after it's glued up, no matter how nice the finished piece looks on day one.

For end grain boards specifically, we're looking for straight-grained walnut, maple, cherry, and other food-safe hardwoods. The boards are cut into strips, flipped on end, and re-glued so the end grain faces up — that's what gives an end grain board its checkerboard look and makes it easier on your knife edge than a regular face-grain board.

Once a blank is glued up, it gets flattened, sanded through several grits, and finished with a food-safe mineral oil and beeswax blend. From rough lumber to a finished board usually takes a little over a week, most of that time spent waiting on glue and finish to cure properly rather than active shop time.

If you've got a specific wood species in mind for a custom board, mention it when you reach out — availability varies, but we're always happy to talk through options.
